What the numbers mean

How it works

This page explains what A Tiempo? can measure, how each result is calculated and where the source data has limits.

01

What can this project measure?

The source provides a scheduled time and an actual runway time. For a departure, the actual value is the takeoff time (ATD). For an arrival, it is the landing time (ATA). A Tiempo? compares those values with the corresponding scheduled time.

The dump does not contain departure off-block time: all 269,377 departure records have an empty blockoff field. This means the departure result is not the same metric an airline calculates from AOBT and SOBT. Taxi-out time is part of the difference shown here.

The application does not subtract an estimated taxi time because that would create a number the source cannot support.

02

What does “within 15 minutes” mean?

A usable flight is within 15 minutes when its actual takeoff or landing time is no more than 15 minutes after its scheduled time. An early flight has a negative difference and also counts.

03

Cancellation and NO OPERA

Only the source status Cancelled counts as a cancellation. NO OPERA is separate. It is not treated as cancelled, within 15 minutes or delayed, and is removed from both rate denominators.

04

Invalid and extreme values

Records without both times cannot contribute to the timing result. Differences whose absolute value exceeds six hours are excluded because they are likely date-parsing or source-data errors.

05

How are the rates calculated?

Within-15-minute rate flights within 15 minutes ÷ all usable flights × 100
Cancellation rate cancelled observations ÷ scheduled observations × 100

“Scheduled observations” excludes NO OPERA. “Usable observations” also excludes cancellations, missing times and extreme values.

06

Why separate takeoffs and landings?

One journey can produce a takeoff record at the origin and a landing record at the destination. Those events answer different questions, so A Tiempo? always shows them separately.

07

Performance labels

“Often near schedule” means at least 80% were within 15 minutes. “Mixed timing" means 65% to 79.9%. Below 65% is labelled “Often more than 15 min late”.

08

Aircraft information

When a model is present, reports list the aircraft most commonly recorded in the selected historical observations. This is context, not a confirmation of the aircraft assigned to a future flight: airlines can change equipment at any time.

09

Source and limitations

Historical records came from a Failbondi data dump dated July 29, 2026, based on public Aeropuertos Argentina information. The source does not guarantee completeness. The dataset includes only flights arriving in or departing from Argentina; it does not cover flights operated entirely outside the country. Results describe past records; they are not a promise about future performance.
