What the numbers mean

How it works

This page explains what A Tiempo? can measure, how each result is calculated and where the source data has limits. It draws on 417,278 airport observations recorded from {{ first_date }} through {{ last_date }}.

01

What is being measured?

For departures, A Tiempo? compares the actual takeoff time (ATD) with the scheduled departure time (STD). Because the source does not include the time an aircraft leaves the gate, this difference also includes taxi-out time. It is therefore not the same as an airline's off-block punctuality metric (AOBT compared with SOBT).

When a route's departure is outside the Argentine airport dataset, the report can use its arrival instead. In that case it compares landing time (ATA) with scheduled arrival time (STA). This measures landing on the runway, not arrival at the gate.

02

What counts as near schedule?

A flight is within the project threshold when it takes off or lands no more than 30 minutes after its scheduled time. An early event also counts. The same threshold is used for departures and arrivals so results remain easy to compare. This is a project rule, not the airline industry's D15 standard.

03

What about cancellations and diversions?

Only records marked Cancelled by the source count as cancellations. They have their own rate and do not enter the schedule performance calculation.

A DIVERTED flight is not a cancellation, but it did not complete the scheduled movement as planned. It therefore counts as an analysed flight outside the 30-minute threshold, regardless of any time attached to that status. Diversions are also shown separately in the report.

NO OPERA records are excluded from both calculations. Other statuses need an actual event time to be analysed.

04

How are the rates calculated?

Within-threshold rate flights within 30 minutes ÷ analysed flights × 100
Cancellation rate cancelled observations ÷ scheduled observations × 100

Analysed flights have either a valid event time or a DIVERTED status. Cancellations and NO OPERA records are not part of this denominator. Records that appear to operate more than six hours early are excluded as likely date mismatches in the source.

The typical difference shown in reports is the median of valid time differences, so an isolated extreme value does not dominate the result. Diversions do not enter this median because they did not complete the scheduled movement normally.

05

What does the aircraft information mean?

The aircraft models are the ones most often recorded in the selected historical period. They provide context only: an airline can change the aircraft assigned to a future flight at any time.

06

Source and limitations

The historical records come from a Failbondi data dump downloaded on July 29, 2026. Failbondi publishes its source code on GitHub and obtains flight-status data from Aeropuertos Argentina.

The source does not guarantee complete or error-free coverage. It only includes flights observed at Argentine airports, which is why some international routes are measured from their arrival. Invalid airport codes are omitted from route lists. Results describe the available past observations; they do not predict a future flight or guarantee its performance.
