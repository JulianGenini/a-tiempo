What the numbers mean

How it works

This page explains what A Tiempo? can measure, how each result is calculated and where the source data has limits. It draws on 417,278 airport observations recorded from {{ first_date }} through {{ last_date }}.

01

What can this project measure?

The source provides scheduled times and actual runway times. For a departure, A Tiempo? compares takeoff time (ATD) with scheduled departure time (STD). For a route search, the application first uses departures recorded at the requested origin. If no matching departure is available, it uses the arrival recorded at the requested destination, comparing landing time (ATA) with scheduled arrival time (STA). In most cases, this fallback represents an international inbound service whose departure was not observed in the Argentine-airport dataset.

The included database does not contain a departure off-block time. This means the departure result is not the same metric an airline calculates from AOBT and SOBT. Taxi-out time is part of the difference shown here.

The application does not subtract an estimated taxi time because that would create a number the source cannot support.

Arrival results use landing rather than arrival at the gate, so they are also a runway-based measure and not the airline's in-block metric.

02

What are the timing thresholds?

A departure is within the displayed threshold when it takes off no more than 30 minutes after STD. The wider threshold accounts for the fact that ATD includes normal taxi-out time; it is a transparent project rule, not the airline industry's D15 measure.

An inbound arrival is within the threshold when it lands no more than 30 minutes after STA. Using the same threshold for both events keeps results easy to interpret and compare. An early takeoff or landing has a negative difference and also counts.

03

Cancellation and NO OPERA

Only the source status Cancelled counts as a cancellation. NO OPERA is separate. It is not treated as cancelled, within the applicable threshold or delayed, and is removed from both rate denominators.

Other source statuses are not automatically excluded. When they have a valid timing difference, they can contribute to timing metrics; this includes, for example, DIVERTED records with valid data.

04

Invalid and extreme values

Records without both times cannot contribute to the timing result. Differences whose absolute value exceeds six hours are excluded because they are likely date-parsing or source-data errors.

Placeholder airport values such as --I, -AR or -BR are not valid IATA codes. Their observations can still contribute to timing metrics when the times are valid, but the placeholders are excluded from route coverage and identified in the report.

05

How are the rates calculated?

Within-threshold rate flights within the event threshold ÷ all usable flights × 100
Cancellation rate cancelled observations ÷ scheduled observations × 100

“Scheduled observations” excludes NO OPERA. “Usable observations” also excludes cancellations, missing times and extreme values.

06

How is a flight number reconstructed?

Some source rows repeat one flight number across later events in the same daily rotation. A flight-number search therefore selects the earliest scheduled event for each date and keeps the movement type recorded most frequently. This supports departures from Argentina and inbound arrivals, most often when the foreign departure is outside the dataset. The rule produces at most one observation per flight number and service date. It uses the complete stored history before the selected date period is applied, so changing the period changes the sample but not the event that the flight number represents.

When a flight number appears on more than one route, directions recorded on only one date are excluded as isolated source anomalies. If the database contains only one observation for a flight, it is preserved and the report labels the sample as insufficient.

A route report first looks for a departure at its origin. If none is recorded, it uses the arrival recorded at its destination. In most cases, this represents an inbound international route because its foreign departure is outside the dataset. Airline reports remain departure-based and use every matching departure.

07

How are the performance labels assigned?

The label is based on the percentage of usable observations that were within the 30-minute timing threshold. The scale is the same for departures and arrivals.

Usually near schedule
80% to 100% within the threshold.
Mixed schedule performance
65% to 79.9% within the threshold.
Often more than X minutes late
Below 65% within the threshold. X is 30 minutes.
Insufficient data
Fewer than 10 usable observations; no performance label is treated as reliable.

Early takeoffs or landings count as within the threshold. For example, a departure marked “Usually near schedule” has at least 80% of its usable observations taking off no more than 30 minutes after STD.

08

Aircraft information

Reports list the aircraft models most commonly recorded in the selected historical observations. Repeated identical model names are normalized; ambiguous values that join different models are omitted rather than guessed. This is context, not a confirmation of the aircraft assigned to a future flight: airlines can change equipment at any time.

09

Source and limitations

Historical records came from a Failbondi data dump dated July 29, 2026. The source does not guarantee completeness. The dataset includes only flights observed at Argentine airports. Route results first use a departure at the requested origin and otherwise fall back to the arrival at the requested destination, most commonly for international inbound routes. They describe past records and use a conservative reconstruction for flight-number searches; they are not a promise about future performance.
