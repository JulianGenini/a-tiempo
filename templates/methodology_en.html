What the numbers mean

How it works

This page explains what A Tiempo? can measure, how each result is calculated and where the source data has limits. It draws on 417,278 airport observations recorded from {{ first_date }} through {{ last_date }}.

01

What is being measured?

For departures, A Tiempo? compares the actual takeoff time (ATD) with the scheduled departure time (STD). Because the database only reports the takeoff and landing times observed by the airport, not the off-block time used by airlines to assess flight performance, the comparisons must be made using the observed takeoff and landing times available in the source. As a result, the difference between ATD and STD for departures may include taxi-out time and is not an airline punctuality metric based on AOBT and SOBT.

When no departure is recorded at the origin airport, the report can use the arrival recorded at the destination airport instead. This occurs, for example, with inbound routes whose origin airport is outside the Argentine airport dataset. In that case, it compares landing time (ATA) with scheduled arrival time (STA). The measurement reflects landing on the destination airport's runway, not the moment the flight reaches the gate.

02

When is a flight considered on time?

A flight is considered on time when it takes off or lands no more than 30 minutes after its scheduled time. The same threshold is used for departures and arrivals so results remain easy to compare.

03

What about cancellations and diversions?

Records marked as cancelled by the source count as cancellations. They have their own rate and do not enter the punctuality calculation.

A diverted flight is not a cancellation, but it did not complete the scheduled movement as planned. It therefore counts as an analysed flight outside the 30-minute threshold. Diversions are also shown separately in the report.

No opera records are excluded from both calculations.

04

How are the rates calculated?

Within-threshold rate flights within 30 minutes ÷ analysed flights × 100
Cancellation rate cancelled observations ÷ scheduled observations (excluding no opera) × 100

To calculate punctuality, the analysis uses flights with a recorded actual time. Diversions are also included among the analysed flights, but count as being outside the 30-minute threshold. Cancellations and no opera records are not part of this calculation. Records without a valid event time are also excluded, as are cases that appear to be more than six hours early because they may reflect a date error in the source.

When a report shows a typical difference, it refers to the median difference between the observed and scheduled times: the middle value when all results are ordered. This keeps one exceptional case from distorting the result. Diversions are not included because they did not complete the scheduled movement normally.

05

What does the aircraft information mean?

The aircraft models are the ones most often recorded in the selected historical period. They provide context only: an airline can change the aircraft assigned to a future flight at any time.

06

Source and limitations

The historical records come from a Failbondi data dump downloaded on July 29, 2026. Failbondi publishes its source code on GitHub and obtains flight-status data from Aeropuertos Argentina.

The source does not guarantee complete or error-free coverage. It only includes flights observed at Argentine airports, which is why some international routes are measured from their arrival. Invalid airport codes are omitted from route lists. Results describe the available past observations; they do not predict a future flight or guarantee its performance.
